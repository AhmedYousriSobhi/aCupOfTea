The photo that turned to static

Blur multiplies each frequency by H; noise is added on top. Dividing by a small H multiplies the noise too, and Wiener stops before that happens.

What does dividing by the blur do to the noise?

At one frequency the blur leaves |H| of the signal, and the noise there is N. The inverse filter divides by H; Wiener divides with a safety term K.

What this assumes
The blur is a horizontal box of length a applied circularly (the picture wraps like wallpaper) and the noise is Gaussian with the standard deviation you pick, drawn from a seeded generator, so a run repeats. The observed picture is not clipped to 0 to 255, matching the model g = h * f + η. The inverse filter leaves out any frequency the blur erased exactly (|H| below 10-6). The Wiener filter uses a single constant K, tuned by you. The single-frequency calculator treats the noise there as N = 1.
