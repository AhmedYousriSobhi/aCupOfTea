Storage and Fabric for AI Clusters - Overview

A two-part series on keeping thousands of GPUs fed - parallel filesystems, small files and checkpoint storms, then RDMA, InfiniBand, RoCE and GPUDirect.

A two-part series on the least glamorous way to waste a GPU: making it wait for data. By the end you'll know how parallel filesystems and RDMA fabrics keep 10,000 GPUs fed, and how to tell when they don't.

The claim, and what's missing from it

A common summary of AI infrastructure says: scaling to 10,000+ GPUs needs data ingestion built on high-performance distributed filesystems such as Lustre or GPFS, paired with InfiniBand or RDMA networks, to prevent GPU starvation.

True, and incomplete in three useful ways:

  • Bandwidth isn't the usual bottleneck; metadata is. Millions of small files starve GPUs on storage with petabytes free. (Part 1)
  • Checkpoints, not datasets, set the peak. Meta's Llama 3 paper names bursty checkpoint writes as a major storage challenge. (Part 1)
  • "InfiniBand or RDMA" is not either/or. InfiniBand is one way to run RDMA; RoCE is another, and Llama 3 405B trained on RoCE. And the last hop into GPU memory needs GPUDirect. (Part 2)
